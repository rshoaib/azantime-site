About Azan Time

Who makes it, and how the numbers on this site are worked out

Azan Time is a free prayer-times app for Android and this companion website. Both are made by Rizwan, an independent developer who publishes apps under the name OVC Tech.

What this website does

  • City prayer times. Each city page shows Fajr, Sunrise, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib and Isha month by month, computed from the city's coordinates and time zone.
  • Qibla direction. The bearing to the Kaaba from each city, from the great-circle formula.
  • Hijri dates. The Hijri converter converts between Gregorian and Hijri dates.

How the times are calculated

Prayer times come from the position of the sun. The pages use the NOAA solar-position equations to find, for each day, the moment the sun reaches the angle each prayer is defined by: the twilight angle for Fajr and Isha, the sun's centre crossing the meridian for Dhuhr, the shadow-length rule for Asr, and sunset for Maghrib.

The twilight angles differ between authorities, so every city page names the convention it uses, normally the one most mosques in that country follow: for example Muslim World League, ISNA, Umm al-Qura University (Makkah), Egyptian General Authority of Survey, University of Islamic Sciences (Karachi), Diyanet, JAKIM, MUIS or Kemenag. Where a country has no single standard, the page says so and uses an 18°/17° convention. Asr is shown with the standard (Shafi'i, Maliki, Hanbali) shadow factor, or the Hanafi factor where that is the local practice, and each page shows how many minutes apart the two are.

Times are given in standard time. Where daylight saving applies, add one hour. At high latitudes in summer the sun may never reach the Fajr or Isha angle; the pages say when that happens instead of inventing a time, because mosques there use different rules.

The Hijri converter uses the Umm al-Qura calendar. The real start of an Islamic month depends on moon sighting where you live, so a date can differ by a day from your local announcement.

Please check with your mosque

Calculated times are a close guide, not a religious ruling. Your local mosque's timetable takes precedence, especially for Fajr, Isha and the start of Ramadan and Eid.

Corrections

If a time, a method or a Qibla bearing looks wrong for your city, please tell me. Include the city and the timetable you compare against. I correct the page and note the change.
