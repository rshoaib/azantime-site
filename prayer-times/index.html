How to use these pages

  1. Find your country above and tap your city. Country headings with a link open a country page that compares its cities.
  2. The city page shows today's Fajr, Sunrise, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib and Isha in that city's local time, worked out in your browser for today's date.
  3. Below that are the times on the 15th of every month, the Qibla bearing, the calculation method, and how far apart the standard and Hanafi Asr times are.
  4. Compare with your mosque's timetable. Where they differ, follow your mosque.

Worked example: Karachi, 15 June

Karachi uses the University of Islamic Sciences, Karachi method (Fajr and Isha at 18° below the horizon) with the Hanafi Asr. On 15 June 2027 that gives Fajr 04:13, Sunrise 05:42, Dhuhr 12:32, Asr 17:15, Maghrib 19:22 and Isha 20:51, so a fast from Fajr to Maghrib lasts 15 h 09 min. The Qibla from Karachi is 267.7° from true north, almost due west. London on the same day has no Fajr or Isha by sun angle at all: the sun never gets 18° below the horizon, which is why UK timetables differ between mosques in summer.

Questions

Why do two websites give different Fajr times for my city?

Usually because they use different twilight angles (for example 18° versus 15°), a different Asr convention, or a high-latitude rule. Each city page names the method it uses so you can compare like with like.

Do the times include daylight saving time?

Today's times on each city page are shown in the city's local clock time, daylight saving included. The year ranges on the country pages are in standard time.

Are these times accurate enough to pray by?

They are calculated from the sun's position at the city's coordinates, so they should match a mosque using the same method to within a few minutes; outlying districts can differ a little more. Your mosque's timetable takes precedence.

How Prayer Times Are Calculated

Prayer times are determined by the position of the sun relative to your location. Fajr and Isha depend on the angle of the sun below the horizon — these angles vary between the major calculation schools. Azan Time supports eight of the most common methods used worldwide:

  • Muslim World League — the global default, widely used in Europe and non-Arab regions
  • ISNA — Islamic Society of North America, standard across US and Canada
  • Umm al-Qura — official method in Saudi Arabia
  • Karachi — University of Islamic Sciences, common in Pakistan, India, Bangladesh
  • Dubai — UAE standard
  • Qatar — General Authority of Islamic Affairs
  • Kuwait — Ministry of Awqaf & Islamic Affairs
  • Egyptian General Authority — common in Egypt, Syria, Iraq, Lebanon

Always use the method followed by your local masjid. If you're unsure, ask your imam — the differences between methods are usually only a few minutes.
