About Prayer Times in Berlin

Muslims in Berlin observe five daily prayers: Fajr before sunrise, Dhuhr after midday, Asr in the afternoon, Maghrib at sunset, and Isha at night. The exact times vary slightly throughout the year based on the sun's position and the calculation method used by your local masjid.

Which calculation method Berlin uses

Prayer times are not a single agreed set of numbers. Fajr and Isha depend on how far the sun sits below the horizon, and different authorities pick different angles, so two apps can both be correct and still disagree by several minutes. In Germany the convention most widely followed is Muslim World League, and that is what timetables printed locally will usually match. If Azan Time differs from the board at your masjid, the calculation method is almost always the reason — set it to match and the times line up.

Asr in Berlin: the two calculations

Asr is the prayer where the difference is largest and most noticeable. The standard calculation begins Asr when an object’s shadow equals its own length; the Hanafi calculation waits until the shadow reaches twice that. In practice the Hanafi time falls roughly 45 minutes to an hour and a half later, and the gap widens in winter. Mosques in Berlin generally follow both conventions, depending on the mosque. This single setting explains most “my app says a different Asr” confusion.

Qibla direction from Berlin

From Berlin the Kaaba lies on a bearing of 136.7° — roughly south-east — measured as a great-circle direction from the city’s coordinates (52.52, 13.405). A compass needle points to magnetic north rather than true north, so a phone compass may read a few degrees off depending on local declination.

Why Berlin times vary more in summer

Berlin sits at 52.52° north. Around midsummer the sun never drops far enough below the horizon for true astronomical twilight to end, so there is no angle at which Fajr or Isha can be calculated in the ordinary way. Every prayer app applies an approximation for those weeks — commonly the midpoint of the night, a fixed one-seventh of the night, or the nearest latitude at which the angle still occurs. Different apps choose differently, which is why summer Isha times in Berlin can differ by over an hour between two apps that agree perfectly in winter.
