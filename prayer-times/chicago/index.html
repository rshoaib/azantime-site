Which calculation method Chicago uses

Prayer times are not a single agreed set of numbers. Fajr and Isha depend on how far the sun sits below the horizon, and different authorities pick different angles, so two apps can both be correct and still disagree by several minutes. In United States the convention most widely followed is ISNA (Islamic Society of North America), and that is what timetables printed locally will usually match. If Azan Time differs from the board at your masjid, the calculation method is almost always the reason — set it to match and the times line up.

Asr in Chicago: the two calculations

Asr is the prayer where the difference is largest and most noticeable. The standard calculation begins Asr when an object’s shadow equals its own length; the Hanafi calculation waits until the shadow reaches twice that. In Chicago the Hanafi time comes 74 minutes later than the standard time on 15 June and 36 minutes later on 15 December — the gap is larger in June, when the long northern summer afternoon stretches it out. Mosques in Chicago generally follow both conventions, depending on the masjid. This single setting explains most “my app says a different Asr” confusion.
