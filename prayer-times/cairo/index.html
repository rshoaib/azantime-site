About Prayer Times in Cairo

Muslims in Cairo observe five daily prayers: Fajr before sunrise, Dhuhr after midday, Asr in the afternoon, Maghrib at sunset, and Isha at night. The exact times vary slightly throughout the year based on the sun's position and the calculation method used by your local masjid.

Which calculation method Cairo uses

Prayer times are not a single agreed set of numbers. Fajr and Isha depend on how far the sun sits below the horizon, and different authorities pick different angles, so two apps can both be correct and still disagree by several minutes. In Egypt the convention most widely followed is Egyptian General Authority of Survey, and that is what timetables printed locally will usually match. If Azan Time differs from the board at your masjid, the calculation method is almost always the reason — set it to match and the times line up.

Asr in Cairo: the two calculations

Asr is the prayer where the difference is largest and most noticeable. The standard calculation begins Asr when an object’s shadow equals its own length; the Hanafi calculation waits until the shadow reaches twice that. In Cairo the Hanafi time comes 77 minutes later than the standard time on 15 June and 42 minutes later on 15 December — the gap is larger in June, when the long northern summer afternoon stretches it out. Mosques in Cairo generally follow the standard calculation, though Hanafi timings are also published. This single setting explains most “my app says a different Asr” confusion.

Qibla direction from Cairo

From Cairo the Kaaba lies on a bearing of 136.1° — roughly south-east — measured as a great-circle direction from the city’s coordinates (30.0444, 31.2357). A compass needle points to magnetic north rather than true north, so a phone compass may read a few degrees off depending on local declination.
