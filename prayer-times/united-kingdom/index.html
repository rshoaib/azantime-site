About Prayer Times in the UK

The UK sits at a high latitude (51–54°N), which means during summer the sun never dips far enough below the horizon for a true astronomical Fajr or Isha. Most British mosques use the Muslim World League (MWL) method with a "middle of the night" or "one-seventh of the night" approximation for Isha during those months — Azan Time applies the same rule automatically.

Times switch between GMT (winter) and BST (summer) automatically. The Qibla from the UK points roughly southeast — about 119° from London. Asr typically follows the Shafi'i calculation, though some Deobandi-affiliated mosques use the Hanafi rule (Azan Time lets you choose).

Questions about prayer times in the UK

Which way is the Qibla from the UK?

Between 118° and 119° from true north, depending on the city (see the table). These are true-north bearings; a phone compass shows magnetic north unless it corrects for declination.

Which calculation method is used for the UK?

There is no single national standard (mosques differ), so the pages use an 18°/17° twilight convention, with the standard Asr time. Each city page states how many minutes apart the standard and Hanafi Asr times are, and names its method.

Why is there no Fajr time in June for some cities in the UK?

In mid-summer the sun does not sink far enough below the horizon for the Fajr and Isha twilight angles to be reached, so no time can be calculated by angle. Mosques then use a high-latitude rule (for example one-seventh of the night or the nearest day with a true Fajr); the city pages say when this happens instead of inventing a time.
